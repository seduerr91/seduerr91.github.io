A `22L` running vest is where overnight fastpacking starts to feel realistic instead of extreme.

You still need to care about volume, weight, and weather, but `22L` gives you enough room to stop playing pure survival Tetris.

This is the kind of setup that works best when:

- The weather window is good
- You only need one night out
- You want to move fast and keep the pack close to the body
- You still want a real sleep system and basic safety margin

If the forecast is ugly, the water carries are long, or you want more camp comfort, I would still size up rather than force `22L` to cover every situation.

## The Reality of 22L

The main constraint is still **compressed volume**, not just base weight.

But `22L` is a very different game from `15L`.

You can carry a one-night kit that feels intentionally light rather than stripped down to the edge.

That means a realistic `22L` overnight setup can usually support:

- Down insulation that compresses well
- A real bivy, small tarp, or compact shelter
- A short pad or even a narrow full-length pad
- Slightly more clothing margin
- More food flexibility
- A small stove if the rest of the kit stays disciplined

It is still not normal backpacking. It is still fastpacking. But it is a version that makes sense for more people.

## What 22L Actually Buys You

| Upgrade from `15L` | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Real shelter options | You do not have to rely on only the tiniest bivy-style setup |
| Warmer sleep system | A `20F-30F` down quilt is much more realistic |
| Better pad choices | Sleep quality improves fast when you stop forcing the smallest pad possible |
| Modest cooking option | A tiny stove and pot become possible if the rest of the kit stays compact |
| More food margin | You can bring a more normal dinner and breakfast instead of pure snack survival |
| Better weather margin | An extra layer or slightly more protective shelter fits without wrecking the whole system |

## My Honest Take

A true `22L` overnight setup is easiest in:

- Summer
- Shoulder season with a reasonable forecast
- Known routes
- One-night pushes
- Trips where you want to move a lot and camp simply

It is much harder in:

- Long wet forecasts
- Cold alpine nights
- Trips with required bear canisters
- Multi-night food carries
- Long wet stretches
- Trips where you care a lot about camp comfort

The biggest tradeoff is still shelter and sleep volume. `22L` gives you more margin, but a bulky synthetic bag, thick pad, and roomy tent will still blow up the plan fast.

## The Two Versions That Actually Make Sense

### 1. Fast and Light No-Cook

This is the version I would choose most often.

| Category | What I Would Bring |
|---|---|
| Pack | `22L` running vest |
| Shelter | Compact bivy, tarp, or small single-wall shelter |
| Sleep insulation | `20F-30F` down quilt |
| Pad | Short inflatable or narrow full-length pad |
| Clothing | Worn running kit, puffy, rain shell, spare socks, thin beanie or buff |
| Water | Two front soft flasks plus a filter and optional extra bottle |
| Food | One dinner, one breakfast, trail snacks, no stove |
| Safety | Headlamp, phone, small battery, mini first aid, repair tape |

This is the version that feels properly fast and light.

### 2. Small Stove and More Comfort

This is the version that spends some of the extra `22L` room on a better night.

| Category | More Comfortable Approach |
|---|---|
| Shelter | Small tarp or compact tent |
| Sleep insulation | Warmer down quilt |
| Pad | Better inflatable pad |
| Kitchen | Tiny stove, `550 ml` pot, lighter, spoon |
| Clothing | Puffy, rain shell, spare socks, beanie, maybe thin gloves |
| Food | Simple hot dinner and coffee or tea |
| Comfort extras | Tiny pillow or small sit pad |

This is still not luxurious, but it is much nicer than the old `15L` game.

## What I Would Actually Pack

Here is the most realistic one-night `22L` kit I can defend.

### Sleep and Shelter

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| `20F-30F` down quilt | `22L` makes a warmer quilt much more realistic |
| Compact shelter | Small tarp, bivy, or compact solo shelter |
| Short or narrow pad | This is where better sleep becomes possible |
| Small pillow | Optional, but much easier to justify now |

### Worn Clothing

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Running shirt | Wear it |
| Running shorts or tights | Wear them |
| Hat | Wear it |
| Socks | Wear one pair |
| Trail shoes | Obviously |

### Packed Clothing

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Puffy | Still one of the highest-value items in the pack |
| Rain shell | Mandatory |
| Spare socks | One pair |
| Thin beanie or buff | Easy warmth for very little space |
| Sleep shirt or thin base layer | Optional, but now much more plausible |

Anything beyond that starts to feel expensive in `22L`, especially if the shelter is not tiny.

### Food and Water

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Two front flasks | Usually the easiest vest carry |
| Small filter | Soft-flask-compatible systems work best here |
| Optional extra bottle | Useful for dry stretches or camp water |
| Dinner | No-cook works best, but a tiny stove dinner is possible |
| Breakfast | Bagel, oatmeal, coffee, bar, or a simple trail breakfast |
| Trail snacks | Enough for the moving hours, not an overflowing food bag |

For food ideas that fit this style, see [Backpacking Meals From a Grocery Store](/blog/backpacking-meals-from-a-grocery-store).

### Safety and Small Stuff

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Headlamp | Non-negotiable |
| Phone with offline maps | Primary navigation |
| Small power bank | One-night size only |
| Tiny first aid kit | Blister care, meds, tape |
| Mini repair tape | Always useful |
| Emergency communication | Optional, but easier to justify in `22L` |
| Trowel or wag bag | Depends on area and rules |

## How I Would Pack the Vest

The packing order matters almost as much as the gear list.

### Main Compartment

| Priority | Item |
|---|---|
| Bottom | Quilt in a dry bag or pack liner |
| Middle | Shelter and pad |
| Middle | Puffy and sleep clothes |
| Middle | Dinner and breakfast food |
| Top | Rain shell and any layer you may need quickly |

### Back Panel or Outside Carry

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Foam pad or sit pad | Outside if needed, but I would rather keep the system internal if possible |
| Trekking poles | If not in hand, use the vest's pole carry |
| Wet shelter | Nice to separate after the first morning |

### Front Pockets

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Two soft flasks | Primary water carry |
| Snacks | Easy access while moving |
| Phone | One front zip pocket or chest pocket |
| Filter | Quick refill access |
| Headlamp | Easy to reach late in the day |
| Gloves or buff | Good to grab without unpacking everything |

### Tiny Zipper Pockets

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Mini first aid | Keep it compressed |
| Battery and cable | Separate from food if possible |
| ID, card, key | Obvious essentials |

## What I Would Leave at Home

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Camp shoes | Still not worth it here |
| Big cook kit | If I cook at all, it should be tiny |
| Duplicate layers | `22L` is better, but it is not a free-for-all |
| Heavy fleece plus puffy | Pick the better system for the forecast |
| Huge power bank | One night does not need it |
| Bulky toiletries | Shrink everything |
| Big tent | Defeats the point of the pack size |

## The Simplest Possible One-Night Food Strategy

If I were trying to make `22L` work cleanly, I would still keep food simple.

| Meal | What I Would Bring |
|---|---|
| Dinner | Tortilla + tuna or salami, or a single compact hot meal if I bring a stove |
| Evening | Tea or instant cider only if the stove is already coming |
| Breakfast | Bar, bagel, oatmeal, or coffee and snacks |
| While moving | Jerky, candy, bars, nuts, dried fruit |

The cleaner the food system, the better the whole pack rides.

## When I Would Upgrade to a Bigger Pack

I would stop trying to force `22L` and move up if:

- The forecast includes real rain
- Nights are properly cold
- I need a roomier shelter
- I am carrying more than one night of food
- The route has long water carries
- A bear canister is required
- I want noticeably more camp comfort

At that point the bigger pack is not failure. It is just the right tool.

## Final Take

Yes, you can do a very good overnight out of a `22L` running vest.

For me, `22L` is close to the sweet spot for one-night fastpacking because it gives you enough room for a real system without losing the close, runnable feel of a vest.

The trick is still not magic packing. The trick is accepting a specific style of trip:

- One night
- Reasonable weather
- Compact shelter
- Compressed down insulation
- A disciplined clothing list
- Simple food
- Limited luxuries

That is still not traditional backpacking. It is fastpacking with sleep gear, just with a lot more sanity than `15L`.

And if you embrace that honestly, `15L` becomes realistic.
