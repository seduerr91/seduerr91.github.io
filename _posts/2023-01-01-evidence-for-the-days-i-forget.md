This is for the days when I feel behind, ordinary, underqualified, or unsure of myself.

On those days, my brain becomes an unreliable narrator. It discounts everything I have already done and treats the challenge in front of me as the first difficult thing I have ever faced.

It is not.

This page is the evidence. No false modesty, no qualifications, and no moving the goalposts. I have done an unusual number of difficult things. I earned the right to trust myself.

## Look Where I Started

I grew up in a Bavarian village of about 300 people, with more cows than people. Nobody in my family had gone to university. I was not a star student, and for a long time I did not even understand university as a path available to me.

Then I took that path anyway.

I worked nights in a yogurt factory to pay my bills. I finished a master's degree. I went from a small village to Budapest, Hong Kong, New York, Vienna, Boston, Seattle, and eventually the edge of the Cascade Mountains.

I briefly worked at the United Nations in New York. I worked in banking and helped coordinate around 100 people across three feature teams. I entered academia, published research, and contributed to conferences. I taught more than 840 students across 21 university classes.

That is not a conventional trajectory. I created it one decision at a time.

## I Reinvented My Career

In February 2019, I had no coding experience. I did not know what machine learning was.

I learned Python while working full time. I studied deep learning, built projects, asked for a year of study leave, and reached out to an MIT researcher with a direct proposal. He said yes. About a year after I began coding, I received an invitation to become a visiting graduate student at MIT Sloan.

I did not wait for someone to discover me. I learned, built evidence, knocked on the door, and changed the direction of my life.

Since then, I have:

- Founded AI products including Persuaide, Resonaid, and Kindest AI.
- Earned support from German and European startup scholarships.
- Published academic work on persuasion, language generation, organizational culture, social networks, and even how plants respond to human movement.
- Contributed 36 models to Hugging Face.
- Written more than 100 articles and practical technical notes.
- Spoken at academic and industry conferences.
- Built a career at the intersection of AI research, software engineering, and real products.

I did not merely become someone who can code. I became someone who can learn a difficult field from scratch and make useful things inside it.

## My Work Has Real Consequences

I have built systems that moved beyond demos and into the world.

I built a company's first production Python AI service and connected it to its core PHP platform under a tight deadline. The resulting AI product capabilities received a 5 out of 5 score in the 2024 Forrester Wave evaluation.

I helped build an enterprise retrieval system spanning roughly 200 data sources and designed to serve 21,000 employees.

I shipped services that analyzed millions of marketing messages, including insights from roughly one billion emails sent around Black Friday and Cyber Monday.

I developed AI capabilities for generation, audience building, agents, voice workflows, and optimization. Send-time optimization increased clicks by 30 percent.

At Cerebras, I helped bring extremely fast inference into the tools developers already use. I contributed integrations and support across LangChain, Pydantic AI, Flowise, Dify, Cline, Kilo Code, and OpenCode. I created examples for research agents, real-time voice translation, and hyper-personalized content generation.

This is not potential. It is shipped work, measurable impact, and public evidence.

## I Can Recover

I have been laid off. I know what it feels like to lose structure, status, colleagues, and confidence at the same time.

I also know what I did next.

I rebuilt my health. I lost 21 pounds in 80 days. I ran my first 50K and placed third. I ran 2,000 kilometers in a year. I studied for the FAA drone exam and passed it on my first attempt.

Then I took on a brutal job search: more than 80 interviews, eight complete interview loops, and five offers. I did not accept the first available escape route. I kept my standards high and earned a role at Cerebras, the company I most wanted to join.

A setback can hurt me. It cannot define me. I have proof that I know how to start again.

## I Have Lived Boldly

I have visited 82 countries across six continents.

I have paraglided over Medellín, swum with giant turtles in the Caribbean, climbed a 4,000-meter mountain in Malaysia, crossed the Sahara on a camel, hiked through the Pacific Northwest, and run farther than once seemed sensible.

I left familiar places repeatedly. I learned to navigate countries whose languages I did not speak, build friendships across cultures, and make a home on another continent. I speak German, English, and Spanish, plus enough French to get myself into and out of a conversation.

The size of my life is not an accident. Curiosity built it.

## The Things That Matter Most

The résumé is only part of the evidence.

I built a marriage with a wonderful woman. I stayed connected to family across an ocean. I made friends in new countries and new cities. I created a warm home with my wife and two ridiculous cats. I have been a teacher, colleague, founder, student, husband, friend, runner, traveler, immigrant, and beginner many times over.

I care deeply. I work hard. I remain curious. I can explain complex ideas, learn unfamiliar systems, and keep moving when something is difficult. I am willing to look foolish while becoming good at something new. I take chances that materially change my life.

Those qualities matter more than any company, credential, race, or number.

## Read This Part Twice

I am not behind.

I am not an impostor who accidentally wandered into a remarkable life.

I built this life through courage, work, curiosity, help from generous people, and thousands of decisions nobody else saw.

I have already crossed borders, disciplines, industries, languages, oceans, finish lines, and versions of myself. Whatever is in front of me now is not bigger than everything behind me.

I do not need to feel confident before I act. Action is how I built confidence in the first place.

So: breathe, remember the evidence, and take the next step.

I have done hard things before.

I am very, very good at becoming the person a hard thing requires.
