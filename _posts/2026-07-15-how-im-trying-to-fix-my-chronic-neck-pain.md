For years I have had recurring neck pain.

Some mornings I can barely turn my head properly to the left or right. Sometimes it improves as the day goes on, only to tighten up again after long hours at the computer.

I do not think there is one dramatic root cause. It feels more like a pileup of small things that have been reinforcing each other for a long time.

This post is my current plan for finally addressing it more seriously instead of just waiting for it to calm down on its own.

This is personal experimentation, not medical advice. If my symptoms get worse, do not improve, or start coming with numbness, tingling, weakness, radiating pain, fever, or severe headaches, I will get evaluated by a qualified clinician.

## What I Think Is Causing It

The most honest answer is probably "several things at once."

### 1. More Than a Decade at a Computer

This is the most obvious contributor.

Years of screens, keyboards, and long work blocks create a very predictable pattern:

- forward head posture
- rounded shoulders
- too much staring at one fixed point
- not enough movement during the day

That combination keeps the neck and upper back under low-grade tension for hours at a time.

### 2. Stress

Stress does not only show up mentally.

It often shows up physically as tightened shoulders, jaw tension, and a neck that never fully relaxes. Even when the work itself is manageable, the body can still hold onto it.

### 3. High Running Volume Without Enough Upper-Body Work

I am running a lot right now, and that is good for many parts of my life, but running alone does not fix posture or upper-back weakness.

High mileage can easily coexist with:

- tight shoulders
- overactive upper traps
- tense arm carriage
- weak scapular stabilizers

In other words, being fit is not the same thing as being balanced.

### 4. Not Enough Mobility

Running gives me a lot of forward motion, but not much rotational movement through the thoracic spine and upper body.

Without regular mobility work, stiffness accumulates quietly and then suddenly starts to feel permanent.

### 5. Sleep Setup

One surprisingly useful clue: redirecting the air conditioner away from my bed helped.

I do not think cold air was the root cause, but it may have been aggravating muscles that were already irritated.

Pillow choice matters too. A bad pillow can undo a lot of good intentions from the rest of the day.

## The 10-Minute Routine I Want to Do Every Day

My goal is not heroic rehab energy. It is consistency.

If I can do a small amount every day, that should help more than occasional ambitious sessions followed by nothing.

### Chin Tucks

This is probably the single highest-value exercise for someone who spends a lot of time at a computer.

- Sit upright
- Look straight ahead
- Pull the chin straight backward into a "double chin"
- Hold for `5` seconds
- Repeat `10` times

The point is to strengthen the deep neck flexors instead of constantly living in a forward-head position.

### Upper Trapezius Stretch

- Sit on one hand
- Tilt the head to the opposite side
- Hold for `30` seconds
- Repeat `2` times per side

### Levator Scapulae Stretch

This one seems especially relevant on days when turning the head is what hurts.

- Turn the head about `45` degrees
- Look down toward the armpit
- Apply gentle downward pressure
- Hold for `30` seconds on each side

### Thoracic Extension

Many neck problems start lower than the neck itself.

- Sit in a chair
- Put hands behind the head
- Lean backward over the top of the chair
- Repeat `10-15` times

A foam roller is even better, but the chair version is easy enough to do during the workday.

### Shoulder Blade Squeezes

- Pull the shoulder blades together
- Avoid shrugging upward
- Hold for `5` seconds
- Repeat `15` times

### Gentle Neck Rotation

- Slowly rotate left and right
- Stop at gentle resistance, not pain
- Repeat `10` times

## Strength Work Matters Too

I do not want this to become a stretch-only project.

If the neck is constantly overworking because the upper back and shoulders are not doing their job, then strength is part of the solution.

The exercises I want `2-3` times per week are:

- face pulls
- resistance band pull-aparts
- rows
- farmer's carries
- dead hangs, if my shoulders tolerate them

The goal is not bodybuilding. The goal is making my posture easier to hold.

## Work Setup Still Matters

No mobility routine will fully compensate for bad ergonomics if I keep repeating the same setup every day.

The checklist I want to follow is simple:

- keep the monitor at eye level
- keep the keyboard close enough that I am not reaching
- keep feet flat on the floor
- stand up every `30-45` minutes
- avoid looking down at a laptop for hours

This is boring advice, but boring advice is often the advice that actually works.

## Running Form Is Part of the Problem or Part of the Fix

While running, I want to pay more attention to the upper body instead of assuming the problem only lives at my desk.

The reminders are:

- relax the shoulders
- unclench the jaw
- avoid tight fists
- let the arms swing naturally

A lot of runners, including me, carry more tension upstairs than we realize.

## Backpacking Sleep Comfort Is Worth Caring About

This part surprised me, but it makes sense: sleep quality matters for the neck almost as much as mobility work does.

I have become less interested in saving every last gram if it means I sleep badly and wake up stiff.

### Pillows

I tried the pure inflatable-pillow approach and did not love it. It felt too much like sleeping on a balloon.

The options that seem more promising are:

- hybrid pillows like the `NEMO Fillo Elite`, `NEMO Fillo`, or `Sea to Summit Aeros Premium`
- the ultralight clothing-in-a-stuff-sack method

That second option is still appealing because it weighs almost nothing and can feel more natural than a fully inflatable pillow.

### Sleeping Pads

The pad matters too.

The two pads I keep coming back to are:

- `Therm-a-Rest NeoAir XLite NXT` for warmth, comfort, and strong all-around performance in the Pacific Northwest
- `NEMO Tensor Elite Short` for lighter summer fastpacking when warmth matters a bit less

My current view is simple: a slightly heavier but better sleep setup is often the correct choice.

## My Actual Action Plan

This is the version I can realistically sustain.

### Every Day

- chin tucks
- neck stretches
- thoracic mobility
- shoulder blade activation

### Every Week

- `2-3` upper-body strength sessions
- keep running
- spend some time hiking with a loaded pack

### Environment and Gear Changes

- keep cold air from blowing directly on my neck at night
- improve pillow setup at home and while backpacking
- use a sleeping pad I actually sleep well on
- stop pretending comfort is irrelevant on overnight trips

## What I’m Hoping For

I do not expect one perfect fix.

I am hoping for something better and more realistic: fewer painful mornings, more comfortable workdays, less stiffness while running, and less anxiety about whether turning my head will hurt today.

Neck pain feels like one of those problems that gets worse when ignored and slowly improves when a lot of small things start going right at the same time.

That is the bet I am making now.
