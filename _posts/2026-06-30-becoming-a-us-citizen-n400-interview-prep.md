# Becoming a U.S. Citizen: My N-400 Interview Prep Guide

As of June 2026, I am waiting for my naturalization interview notice. I filed Form N-400 on December 31, 2025.

USCIS applies the **2025 civics test** to N-400 filings on or after **October 20, 2025**, and that official question bank has `128` questions. Because I filed on **December 31, 2025**, this is the version I need to prepare for.

The good news is that the interview is not just a giant memory game. The process is very structured, and a lot of the preparation is really about being organized, calm, and consistent.

## What USCIS Will Actually Test

Based on the current USCIS interview and test guidance, I should be ready for four things:

- My N-400 application reviewed out loud
- An English speaking check during the interview
- A short English reading test and a short English writing test
- The civics test

For the **2025 civics test**, USCIS says the officer asks up to `20` questions from the `128`-question bank, and I need `12` correct to pass. For English, USCIS evaluates speaking during the interview, then asks me to read `1` out of `3` sentences correctly and write `1` out of `3` sentences correctly.

## My Prep Roadmap While I Wait

### 1. Re-read My Entire N-400

Before I memorize civics facts, I want to know my own application cold.

That means reviewing:

- Every address from the last `5` years
- My work history
- Every trip outside the United States
- Marriage history if relevant
- Every yes/no answer on the form
- Anything that has changed since filing

If the officer asks a question from the form, I do not want to sound surprised by my own paperwork.

### 2. Build One Clean Interview Folder

I want one folder with originals and copies of anything I might reasonably be asked about.

My default folder would include:

- Interview notice once it arrives
- Green card
- Passport and old passports or travel documents
- State ID or driver's license
- Tax transcripts or certified returns
- Proof of Selective Service registration if it applies to me
- Marriage, divorce, or name change documents if they apply
- Certified court records for any citations, arrests, or charges if they apply

For tax records, USCIS guidance is especially clear that these matter. I want to have the last `5` years ready, or `3` years if the application is based on marriage to a U.S. citizen.

### 3. Practice the English Part Out Loud

The speaking test is not separate from the interview. It is built into the conversation.

That means I want to practice answering in short, clear sentences instead of over-explaining. I also want to practice reading simple USCIS-style sentences and writing simple sentences by hand.

### 4. Study Civics in Small Daily Batches

Trying to cram `128` questions in one weekend is a terrible plan.

A better plan is:

- `15-20` civics questions per day
- One short spoken mock interview each week
- One pass through the full question bank every `1-2` weeks
- A final refresh of the changing answers right before the interview

### 5. Re-check the "Live" Answers Right Before Interview Day

A few civics answers can change with elections or appointments. I should verify these shortly before the interview:

- My U.S. representative
- One current U.S. senator from Washington
- The Speaker of the House
- The President
- The Vice President
- The Chief Justice
- The governor of Washington

## Other Questions I Should Expect Beyond Civics

The officer will usually spend real time on the application itself. These are the areas I want to be ready for.

### Identity, Address, and Basic Background

Possible questions:

- What is your full legal name?
- Have you used any other names?
- Where do you live now?
- How long have you lived there?
- Where do you work?

How I want to prepare:

- Know my current address exactly
- Know my move-in dates
- Know my employer name, job title, and work history from the form
- Keep answers short and factual

### Travel and Physical Presence

Possible questions:

- When was your last trip outside the United States?
- How many total trips did you take?
- How long was your longest trip?
- Have you taken any trips since filing?

How I want to prepare:

- Keep a printed trip list
- Match my answers to my passport stamps and the N-400
- Be ready to explain any longer trip clearly and calmly

### Taxes, Selective Service, and Legal Questions

Possible questions:

- Have you filed your taxes every year?
- Do you owe any overdue taxes?
- Did you register for Selective Service if required?
- Have you ever been cited, arrested, charged, or convicted?

How I want to prepare:

- Bring transcripts and payment records if relevant
- Answer honestly, not strategically
- If something happened, bring the official disposition documents instead of hoping it will not come up

### Marriage, Family, and Paper Trail Questions

Possible questions:

- Are you married?
- What is your spouse's status?
- Do you have children?
- Have you ever changed your name?

How I want to prepare:

- Review dates carefully
- Bring supporting documents if they apply
- Make sure names and dates match across forms, passports, and certificates

### Oath, Constitution, and "Good Citizen" Questions

These are the questions I especially want to answer like a normal thoughtful person, not like a robot.

Possible questions:

- Why do you want to become a U.S. citizen?
- What does the Oath of Allegiance mean to you?
- Are you willing to support and defend the Constitution?
- What does being a good citizen mean?

The main thing I want to remember is that these answers should be **true**, not memorized theater.

A strong honest answer to "What is a good citizen?" could sound like this:

> A good citizen respects the Constitution and the form of government, follows the law, pays taxes honestly, tells the truth, serves on a jury if called, stays informed, and contributes to the community.

A strong honest answer to "Why do you want to become a U.S. citizen?" could sound like this:

> The United States is my home. I want to participate fully in the country where I live, including voting and taking on the responsibilities of citizenship.

A strong honest answer to "Are you willing to support and defend the Constitution?" is simpler:

> Yes. I believe in constitutional self-government and I am willing to take the responsibilities that come with citizenship.

## My Simple Study Schedule

If I am waiting for the interview notice, this is the study rhythm I want:

1. Week 1: Review the full N-400, fix my timeline notes, and gather documents.
2. Week 2: Start daily civics study and practice spoken answers to common interview questions.
3. Week 3: Add English reading and writing practice, plus one full mock interview.
4. Every week after that: Repeat civics, re-check documents, and keep answers short and consistent.
5. Interview week: Re-check the live civics answers and bring the folder.

## The 128 Official Civics Questions

Important note: some official questions have **many** acceptable answers. Below I list one acceptable answer for single-answer questions, and enough acceptable answers for questions that require two, three, or five responses. For anything dynamic or election-sensitive, I should verify the live answer before the interview.

### American Government: Principles of American Democracy

1. **What is the form of government of the United States?** Republic

2. **What is the supreme law of the land?** (U.S.) Constitution

3. **Name one thing the U.S. Constitution does.** Forms the government

4. **The U.S. Constitution starts with the words "We the People." What does "We the People" mean?** Self-government

5. **How are changes made to the U.S. Constitution?** Amendments

6. **What does the Bill of Rights protect?** (The basic) rights of Americans

7. **How many amendments does the U.S. Constitution have?** Twenty-seven (27)

8. **Why is the Declaration of Independence important?** It says America is free from British control.

9. **What founding document said the American colonies were free from Britain?** Declaration of Independence

10. **Name two important ideas from the Declaration of Independence and the U.S. Constitution.** Equality; liberty

11. **The words "Life, Liberty, and the pursuit of Happiness" are in what founding document?** Declaration of Independence

12. **What is the economic system of the United States?** Capitalism

13. **What is the rule of law?** Everyone must follow the law.

14. **Many documents influenced the U.S. Constitution. Name one.** Declaration of Independence

15. **There are three branches of government. Why?** So one part does not become too powerful

### American Government: System of Government

16. **Name the three branches of government.** Legislative, executive, and judicial

17. **The President of the United States is in charge of which branch of government?** Executive branch

18. **What part of the federal government writes laws?** (U.S.) Congress

19. **What are the two parts of the U.S. Congress?** Senate and House (of Representatives)

20. **Name one power of the U.S. Congress.** Writes laws

21. **How many U.S. senators are there?** One hundred (100)

22. **How long is a term for a U.S. senator?** Six (6) years

23. **Who is one of your state's U.S. senators now?** One of Washington's current U.S. senators. I should verify the live answer before the interview.

24. **How many voting members are in the House of Representatives?** Four hundred thirty-five (435)

25. **How long is a term for a member of the House of Representatives?** Two (2) years

26. **Why do U.S. representatives serve shorter terms than U.S. senators?** To more closely follow public opinion

27. **How many senators does each state have?** Two (2)

28. **Why does each state have two senators?** Equal representation (for small states)

29. **Name your U.S. representative.** My current U.S. representative for my address and district.

30. **What is the name of the Speaker of the House of Representatives now?** Check USCIS test updates for the current Speaker of the House.

31. **Who does a U.S. senator represent?** Citizens of their state

32. **Who elects U.S. senators?** Citizens from their state

33. **Who does a member of the House of Representatives represent?** Citizens in their (congressional) district

34. **Who elects members of the House of Representatives?** Citizens from their (congressional) district

35. **Some states have more representatives than other states. Why?** (Because of) the state's population

36. **The President of the United States is elected for how many years?** Four (4) years

37. **The President of the United States can serve only two terms. Why?** (Because of) the 22nd Amendment

38. **What is the name of the President of the United States now?** Check USCIS test updates for the current President.

39. **What is the name of the Vice President of the United States now?** Check USCIS test updates for the current Vice President.

40. **If the president can no longer serve, who becomes president?** The Vice President (of the United States)

41. **Name one power of the president.** Signs bills into law

42. **Who is Commander in Chief of the U.S. military?** The President (of the United States)

43. **Who signs bills to become laws?** The President (of the United States)

44. **Who vetoes bills?** The President (of the United States)

45. **Who appoints federal judges?** The President (of the United States)

46. **The executive branch has many parts. Name one.** President (of the United States)

47. **What does the President's Cabinet do?** Advises the President (of the United States)

48. **What are two Cabinet-level positions?** Secretary of State; Secretary of the Treasury

49. **Why is the Electoral College important?** It decides who is elected president.

50. **What is one part of the judicial branch?** Supreme Court

51. **What does the judicial branch do?** Reviews laws

52. **What is the highest court in the United States?** Supreme Court

53. **How many seats are on the Supreme Court?** Nine (9)

54. **How many Supreme Court justices are usually needed to decide a case?** Five (5)

55. **How long do Supreme Court justices serve?** (For) life

56. **Supreme Court justices serve for life. Why?** To be independent (of politics)

57. **Who is the Chief Justice of the United States now?** Check USCIS test updates for the current Chief Justice.

58. **Name one power that is only for the federal government.** Print paper money

59. **Name one power that is only for the states.** Provide schooling and education

60. **What is the purpose of the 10th Amendment?** (It states that the) powers not given to the federal government belong to the states or to the people.

61. **Who is the governor of your state now?** Washington's current governor. I should verify the live answer before the interview.

62. **What is the capital of your state?** Olympia

### American Government: Rights and Responsibilities

63. **There are four amendments to the U.S. Constitution about who can vote. Describe one of them.** Citizens eighteen (18) and older (can vote).

64. **Who can vote in federal elections, run for federal office, and serve on a jury in the United States?** Citizens

65. **What are three rights of everyone living in the United States?** Freedom of speech; freedom of religion; freedom of assembly

66. **What do we show loyalty to when we say the Pledge of Allegiance?** The United States

67. **Name two promises that new citizens make in the Oath of Allegiance.** Give up loyalty to other countries; defend the (U.S.) Constitution

68. **How can people become United States citizens?** Be born in the United States under the conditions set by the 14th Amendment; naturalize; derive citizenship under conditions set by Congress

69. **What are two examples of civic participation in the United States?** Vote; contact elected officials

70. **What is one way Americans can serve their country?** Vote

71. **Why is it important to pay federal taxes?** Required by law

72. **It is important for all men age 18 through 25 to register for the Selective Service. Name one reason why.** Required by law
