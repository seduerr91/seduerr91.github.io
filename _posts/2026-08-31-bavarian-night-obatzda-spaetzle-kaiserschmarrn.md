This is my kind of comfort-food dinner: put a bowl of **Obatzda** and warm pretzels on the table first, follow it with buttery homemade **Spätzle**, then make **Kaiserschmarrn** while everyone is still sitting around after dinner.

The menu crosses a few borders. Obatzda is Bavarian, Spätzle comes from Swabia and the wider southern German region, and Kaiserschmarrn is an Austrian classic. Together, though, they make one very good Alpine-style dinner at home.

## The Menu

| Course | Dish | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Appetizer | Obatzda with pretzels or rye bread | Make first or one day ahead |
| Main or side | Fresh buttered Spätzle | Make just before dinner |
| Optional easy protein | Grilled sausage or beef hot dogs | Cook while the Spätzle water heats |
| Dessert | Kaiserschmarrn with powdered sugar and fruit compote | Make and serve immediately |

The full menu serves **4 to 6 people**. If I want a low-effort main alongside the Spätzle, I grill one sausage per person. Bratwurst or Weisswurst fits the theme best, but [Kirkland Signature beef hot dogs](https://www.costco.com/p/-/kirkland-signature-beef-hot-dogs-12-links-15-lbs-3-count/100410830?langId=-1) are an easy crowd-size shortcut. Add mustard and sauerkraut and dinner is handled.

## Obatzda

Obatzda is a soft, savory cheese spread with Camembert, butter, onion, sweet paprika, caraway, and just enough wheat beer to loosen it. The texture should be rustic and spreadable, not smooth and runny.

**Serves:** 6 to 8 as an appetizer · **Prep time:** 15 minutes, plus 30 minutes to rest

### Ingredients

- 8 oz ripe Camembert, at room temperature (*Président* is a good choice; *Signature Select* Brie works as a backup)
- 4 oz full-fat block cream cheese, at room temperature (*Philadelphia* or *Lucerne*)
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter, softened (*Lucerne* or *Tillamook*)
- 1 small yellow or red onion, very finely chopped
- 2 tbsp German wheat beer, plus a little more if needed (*Franziskaner* or *Paulaner*; Blue Moon or milk also works)
- 1 tsp sweet paprika, plus more for garnish (*McCormick* is fine; do not use smoked paprika)
- 1/2 tsp caraway seeds, lightly crushed
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
- 2 tbsp finely chopped fresh chives, plus more for garnish
- Soft pretzels, pretzel crisps, rye bread, or sliced radishes for serving

### Instructions

1. Cut the Camembert into chunks. Leave the rind on for a more traditional, rustic texture, or trim some of it away for a smoother spread.
2. Mash the Camembert, cream cheese, and softened butter together with a fork until combined but still slightly chunky.
3. Mix in the onion, paprika, crushed caraway, chives, a small pinch of salt, and several grinds of black pepper.
4. Stir in the beer one tablespoon at a time. Stop when the mixture is easy to spread; it should not pour.
5. Cover and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes so the flavors can settle.
6. Let it stand at room temperature for about 20 minutes before serving. Finish with chives and another dusting of sweet paprika.

Obatzda can be made a day ahead and keeps for up to three days in the refrigerator. If raw onion gets stronger than you like over time, mix in only half and scatter the rest on top just before serving.

## Homemade Spätzle

Spätzle are tender, irregular egg noodles. Their craggy surface is especially good at holding butter, gravy, or cheese. A Spätzle press is fastest, but a colander with large holes works too.

**Serves:** 4 to 6 as a side · **Prep time:** 15 minutes · **Cook time:** 10 minutes

### Ingredients

- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp fine salt, plus more for the cooking water
- 1/4 tsp ground nutmeg, optional
- 4 large eggs
- 1/3 to 1/2 cup whole milk or water
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter
- Chopped parsley or chives, for serving
- Freshly ground black pepper, to taste

### Instructions

1. Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a gentle boil.
2. Whisk the flour, salt, and nutmeg together in a large bowl. Add the eggs and 1/3 cup milk.
3. Beat vigorously with a wooden spoon until the dough is smooth, elastic, and begins to form bubbles. Add the remaining milk a tablespoon at a time if it feels too stiff. It should fall from the spoon in thick, slow ribbons rather than pour like pancake batter.
4. Let the dough rest for 5 to 10 minutes.
5. Working in batches, press the dough through a Spätzle maker or large-holed colander directly into the simmering water. Stir once so the noodles do not clump.
6. When the Spätzle float, cook them for about one more minute. Lift them out with a slotted spoon and drain well.
7. Melt the butter in a large skillet. Toss the cooked Spätzle in the butter for 2 to 3 minutes, until glossy with a few golden edges. Season with black pepper and finish with parsley or chives.

For **Käsespätzle**, fold in about 6 oz of grated Emmentaler or Gruyère while the noodles are hot, then top with deeply browned onions.

## Kaiserschmarrn

Kaiserschmarrn is a fluffy pancake torn into bite-size pieces, lightly caramelized, and covered with powdered sugar. It looks gloriously messy because that is the entire point.

**Serves:** 4 · **Prep time:** 20 minutes · **Cook time:** 15 minutes

### Ingredients

- 2 tbsp raisins, optional
- 1 tbsp dark rum or warm water, optional
- 6 large eggs, separated
- 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 1/2 cups whole milk
- 3 tbsp granulated sugar
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 1/2 tsp finely grated lemon zest
- 1/4 tsp fine salt
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter, divided
- 1 tbsp granulated sugar, for caramelizing
- Powdered sugar and cinnamon, for serving
- Plum compote, applesauce, or berry preserves, for serving

### Instructions

1. Heat the oven to **350°F**. If using raisins, soak them in rum or warm water for 15 minutes, then drain.
2. Whisk the egg yolks, milk, vanilla, and lemon zest together. Add the flour and whisk until mostly smooth.
3. In a clean bowl, beat the egg whites and salt until foamy. Gradually add the 3 tablespoons of sugar and continue beating until glossy medium-stiff peaks form.
4. Fold one-third of the egg whites into the yolk mixture to loosen it, then gently fold in the rest without knocking out too much air.
5. Melt 2 tablespoons of butter in a large oven-safe skillet over medium heat. Pour in the batter and scatter the raisins over the top. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes, until the underside is golden and the edges begin to set.
6. Cut the pancake into four sections with a spatula and flip each section. Move the skillet to the oven and bake for 6 to 8 minutes, until puffed and cooked through.
7. Return the skillet to the stove. Tear the pancake into bite-size pieces with two forks or spatulas. Add the remaining 2 tablespoons of butter, sprinkle with 1 tablespoon of sugar, and toss over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes until the edges caramelize.
8. Dust generously with powdered sugar and a little cinnamon. Serve immediately with plum compote, applesauce, or berry preserves.

No oven-safe skillet? After flipping the sections, cover the pan and cook over low heat until the center is set, then tear and caramelize as written.

## A Low-Stress Cooking Order

1. Make the Obatzda first and refrigerate it.
2. Set the table, prep the sausage toppings, and measure the ingredients for both batters.
3. Take the Obatzda out of the refrigerator 20 minutes before serving.
4. After the appetizer, heat the Spätzle water and grill the sausages at the same time.
5. Mix, boil, and butter the Spätzle immediately before dinner.
6. Wait until after dinner to fold and cook the Kaiserschmarrn batter. It is at its best straight from the skillet.

## Recipe Notes and Inspiration

The Obatzda proportions and technique draw from several Bavarian beer-cheese versions, including [Dirndl Kitchen](https://dirndlkitchen.com/oktoberfest-recipes-part-2-obatzda/), [My Dinner](https://mydinner.co.uk/obatzda/), and [Host the Toast](https://hostthetoast.com/obatzda-bavarian-beer-cheese-dip/). The basic noodle ratio is consistent with [Plated Cravings' German Spätzle](https://platedcravings.com/authentic-easy-german-spaetzle-recipe/), while the Kaiserschmarrn method follows the classic separated-egg approach described by [Austria Tourism](https://www.austria.info/en-gb/recipes/kaiserschmarren/).
