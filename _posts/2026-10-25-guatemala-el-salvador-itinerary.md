If you want to combine big volcanic landscapes, warm Pacific surf, and reliable infrastructure for a few remote work days, pairing **Guatemala and El Salvador** over two weeks is hard to beat.

**February and March** are the absolute sweet spot for this route. It is the peak of the Central American dry season (*Verano*), which translates to crisp, clear skies—crucial when you are hiking up to watch an active volcano—cool mountain nights, and dry, mud-free trails.

Below is a **13-day, two-country itinerary** designed for a group of colleagues: high-reward outdoor days, built-in recovery, solid Wi-Fi for remote work blocks, and a pre-trip checklist so everyone books the right flights and requests the right PTO days.

---

## Why This Route Works

### 1. "That Volcano" Is Acatenango (and Volcán de Fuego)

When people talk about the bucket-list volcano hike in Central America, they are almost always talking about **Volcán Acatenango** outside Antigua, Guatemala. You climb Acatenango not just for its own summit, but for a front-row seat to neighboring **Volcán de Fuego**, one of the most active stratovolcanoes in the world, which regularly sends plumes of ash and glowing lava into the night sky.

Here is the honest terrain breakdown: Acatenango is a steep climb with roughly **5,000 feet of elevation gain**, topping out near **13,000 feet (3,976 m)** on loose volcanic gravel. Hauling a heavy overnight pack at altitude can turn a world-class view into a long, exhausting slog for any group.

The smart play—so the entire team actually enjoys the evening instead of just surviving the climb—is to book a **premium outfitter** (such as **Wicho & Charlie's** or **OX Expeditions**) that maintains insulated **permanent A-frame cabins** at basecamp, and to **treat personal porters as standard issue for everyone**. Offloading sleeping gear, extra layers, and heavy water carries lets everyone hike with a light daypack and save their legs for the views.

### 2. High-Reward, Accessible Adventure in El Salvador

After Acatenango, the second half of the trip shifts to **El Salvador**, where the ratio of scenery to physical grind is extraordinarily friendly. You get a steaming turquoise crater lake (**Volcán Santa Ana**), a wet canyon trek through seven waterfalls (**Siete Cascadas**), cliff jumping in the coastal jungle (**Tamanique**), and warm black-sand beaches in **El Zonte**—all via short, punchy hikes rather than multi-hour altitude marches.

## Phase-by-Phase Breakdown

### Phase 1: Antigua & Acatenango (Days 1–5)

**Antigua** sits in the Panchoy Valley at roughly **5,000 feet (1,530 m)**, ringed by three volcanoes: Agua, Fuego, and Acatenango. Coming straight from sea level in Seattle or San Francisco, spending Thursday night and all of Friday in Antigua is not just sightseeing—it gives everyone 36 hours to adjust to mild elevation and hydrate before stepping onto the Acatenango trailhead on Saturday morning.

* **Day 2 (Friday) Acclimatization:** Keep the pace conversational. Walk the historic center (Santa Catalina Arch, La Merced, the ruins of Catedral de Santiago), visit a local coffee estate like **Filadelfia** or **Finca El Pilar**, and do a final gear check with the outfitter in the afternoon.
* **Day 3 (Saturday) Acatenango Ascent:** After a 1-hour shuttle to La Soledad, the trail climbs steadily through four distinct microclimates: local farm fields, humid cloud forest, temperate pine and alder forest, and finally windswept volcanic scree near basecamp (~11,800 ft). With a porter carrying the overnight load, you only carry water, snacks, a camera, and a windbreaker. By late afternoon you are in camp with hot food and hot drinks, watching **Fuego** boom and send incandescent rock down its flanks across the saddle.
* **Day 4 (Sunday) Sunrise & Descent:** The 4:00 AM summit push adds the final ~1,200 vertical feet on steep ash for a 360-degree sunrise across the Guatemalan volcanic chain all the way to Lake Atitlán and the Pacific. Because basecamp already has the best head-on angle of Fuego, anyone who prefers to sleep in and watch sunrise from the cabins with a mug of coffee misses zero of the main show. The descent back to the van takes ~2–3 hours, putting everyone back in Antigua by midday for hot showers, massages, and a celebratory dinner.
* **Day 5 (Monday) Remote Work in Antigua:** Antigua is one of Latin America's premier remote-work hubs. Boutique hotels and coworking spots (like Impact Hub Antigua or garden cafés such as Fat Cat and Artista de Café) offer low-latency fiber connections and quiet spaces for video calls.

### Phase 2: The Coffee Highlands of El Salvador (Days 6–8)

On Tuesday (Day 6), we transition from Guatemala into western El Salvador's **Ruta de las Flores**, a scenic corridor winding through volcanic highlands between 3,500 and 4,500 feet.

* **Flight vs. Overland Shuttle:** You have two clean options here:
  1. **Door-to-door private shuttle (~5 hours):** Leaves straight from our Antigua hotel, crosses the border at Valle Nuevo / Las Chinamas, and drops us directly in **Juayúa** or **Concepción de Ataco**. Because the Ruta de las Flores is in *western* El Salvador (near the Guatemalan border), driving directly is often just as fast door-to-door as flying via San Salvador's airport in the south.
  2. **45-minute flight (`GUA` → `SAL`):** Quick hop on Avianca or TAG, followed by a ~1.5-hour shuttle northwest into the mountains. Great if you want to skip border-crossing road time.
* **Day 7 (Wednesday) Volcán Santa Ana (Ilamatepec):** Located in Cerro Verde National Park, Santa Ana is El Salvador's highest volcano (7,812 ft / 2,381 m), yet the trail starts high up in the cloud forest, making it a very approachable **4-mile round-trip** with gradual switchbacks. At the rim, you look straight down into a vivid, steaming **emerald-turquoise sulfur lagoon**. Afterward, you can have lunch overlooking **Lake Coatepeque**, a massive volcanic caldera lake just down the mountain.
* **Day 8 (Thursday) Remote Work in the Highlands:** Spend Thursday working from a boutique finca or hotel in Ataco or Juayúa. The climate is spring-like all day, the Pacamara and Bourbon coffee was grown on the slopes outside your window, and evenings wind down with freshly made *pupusas* on the town plaza.

### Phase 3: Waterfalls & the Pacific Coast (Days 9–13)

Friday bridges the mountains and the ocean:

* **Day 9 (Friday) Los Chorros de la Calera (Siete Cascadas):** Guided morning hike out of Juayúa through coffee fincas and jungle canyons linking seven distinct waterfalls, including a guided rappel down one of the cascades and a swim in natural spring pools at the end. Pack water shoes or trail runners that can get soaked. After lunch in Juayúa, a 2-hour shuttle drops out of the mountains to **El Zonte** on the Pacific coast.
* **Day 10–12 (Saturday–Monday) El Zonte & Tamanique:**
  * **El Zonte** is a laid-back, walkable cliffside surf village ("Bitcoin Beach") with boutique oceanfront hotels (like Palo Verde, Puro Surf, or El Zonte Beach Resort), infinity pools, and dramatic black-sand beaches.
  * **Tamanique Waterfalls (Day 11):** A 30-minute drive into the coastal hills behind El Zonte leads to a series of four cascading jungle waterfalls tucked into a canyon—ideal for swimming and optional cliff jumps ranging from easy 10-foot ledges to higher drops. Keep in mind the hike *out* of the canyon is short (~30–40 minutes) but steep and warm, so go early in the morning.
  * **Surf & Buffer Day (Day 12):** Monday gives everyone a flexible day: book a 1-on-1 beginner surf lesson on the sandy beach break, catch up on emails from a shaded palapa, or simply decompress by the ocean before Tuesday's departure.
* **Day 13 (Tuesday) Seamless Departure:** Unlike Guatemala City—where Antigua sits on the far side of heavy urban traffic—El Salvador's international airport (`SAL`) sits right on the coastal plain. From El Zonte, it is a breeze of a **45-minute highway ride** straight to the terminal.

## Tactical Execution Notes

* **Work Infrastructure & Redundancy:** Both Antigua and El Zonte cater heavily to remote tech workers. Boutique hotels and coworking spaces routinely pair fiber lines with **Starlink backup** and backup generators, meaning Monday (Day 5) and Thursday (Day 8)—plus Monday (Day 12) if you are working—are very safe for video syncs.
* **Time Zones:** Both Guatemala and El Salvador stay on **Central Standard Time (`UTC-6`) year-round** and do not observe Daylight Saving Time. In February (and early March before US DST starts), that is **2 hours ahead of Pacific Time (`PT`)**—meaning an 8:00 AM PT standup is a civilized 10:00 AM local time, leaving room for a morning walk or surf session before logging on.
* **Drone Logistics:** If you are bringing a DJI sub-250g or Mavic drone:
  * **Guatemala:** Technically requires temporary registration with the *Dirección General de Aeronáutica Civil* (DGAC), and national parks (including Tikal and certain archaeological zones) restrict drone use without permits. Customs enforcement at `GUA` is inconsistent, so carry the drone in a low-profile carry-on sleeve.
  * **El Salvador:** Generally very relaxed for recreational drone photography along the coast and mountains; just observe standard geo-zones around `SAL` airport and government/military sites.

## Colleague Pre-Flight Checklist

To keep group coordination painless, here is the exact checklist for everyone joining the trip.

### 1. PTO & Vacation Day Math

Because this itinerary straddles **two weekends** and includes **scheduled remote work days**, you do not need to burn two full weeks of PTO:

| Day | Activity Type | PTO Needed? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Thu (Day 1)** | Outbound flight (`SEA` or `SFO` → `GUA`) | **1 PTO Day** *(or 0 if taking a Wednesday night red-eye)* |
| **Fri (Day 2)** | Antigua acclimatization & exploration | **1 PTO Day** |
| **Sat–Sun (Days 3–4)** | Acatenango Volcano overnight | Weekend (0 PTO) |
| **Mon (Day 5)** | Remote Work Day in Antigua | **Remote Work (0 PTO)** |
| **Tue (Day 6)** | Transit to El Salvador (Ruta de las Flores) | **1 PTO Day** *(or half-day if taking an evening flight)* |
| **Wed (Day 7)** | Volcán Santa Ana hike & Lake Coatepeque | **1 PTO Day** |
| **Thu (Day 8)** | Remote Work Day in Juayúa / Ataco | **Remote Work (0 PTO)** |
| **Fri (Day 9)** | Siete Cascadas trek & transfer to El Zonte | **1 PTO Day** |
| **Sat–Sun (Days 10–11)** | El Zonte coast & Tamanique waterfalls | Weekend (0 PTO) |
| **Mon (Day 12)** | Surf / Buffer Day *(or optional Remote Work Day)* | **0 to 1 PTO Day** |
| **Tue (Day 13)** | Return flight (`SAL` → `SEA` or `SFO`) | **1 PTO Day** |

* **Standard Setup:** **6–7 PTO days** + **2–3 Remote Work days** = **13 days on the ground**.
* **Minimum-PTO Setup:** If you take a Wednesday night red-eye down and work remotely on Monday (Day 12) from El Zonte, you can do the entire 13-day trip on **5 PTO days**.

### 2. Flights to Book (Open-Jaw from `SEA` or `SFO`)

**Do not book a round-trip ticket to Guatemala City.** Book a **Multi-City (Open-Jaw)** itinerary:
* **Leg 1 (Outbound):** `SEA` or `SFO` $\rightarrow$ **`GUA`** (La Aurora International Airport, Guatemala City)
* **Leg 2 (Return):** **`SAL`** (El Salvador International Airport, San Salvador) $\rightarrow$ `SEA` or `SFO`

#### Best Routing Options by Hub
* **From San Francisco (`SFO`):**
  * **Outbound (`SFO` → `GUA`):** **Avianca** and **United** offer direct flights (or 1-stop connections via `SAL`, `LAX`, or `IAH`). If you take the Wednesday night nonstop red-eye, you land in `GUA` early Thursday morning and save a full travel day.
  * **Return (`SAL` → `SFO`):** **Avianca** and **United** operate **nonstop flights** between `SAL` and `SFO` (~5.5–6 hours).
* **From Seattle (`SEA`):**
  * **Outbound (`SEA` → `GUA`):** Easy 1-stop connections via **United** (`IAH` or `SFO`), **American** (`DFW` or `MIA`), **Delta** (`ATL` or `LAX`), or **Alaska + Avianca** (`SFO` or `LAX`).
  * **Return (`SAL` → `SEA`):** Fastest 1-stop return is via `SFO`, `LAX`, or `IAH` on United/Avianca, or via `DFW` on American.
* **Pro Tip on Booking:** You can either book a single multi-city ticket on **United** or **Avianca** (both are Star Alliance partners), or book two separate one-way tickets if mixing carriers gives you better departure times. Try to land at `GUA` within a 2-hour window of the group on Day 1 so we can share the private shuttle to Antigua.

### 3. Passports, Visas & Entry Requirements

* **Passport Validity:** Must be valid for **at least 6 months** beyond our return date, with at least **2 blank pages** for stamps. Check your expiration date *before* booking flights.
* **CA-4 Border Agreement:** Guatemala and El Salvador (alongside Honduras and Nicaragua) are part of the **Central America-4 (CA-4) Border Control Agreement**, which allows up to 90 days of visa-free travel across the region for US, Canadian, UK, and EU passport holders.
* **Guatemala Electronic Declaration (`GUA`):** Within 72 hours before flying into Guatemala, fill out the free official digital customs/traveler declaration (*Declaración Jurada Regional de Viajero*) online and save the QR code screenshot to your phone.
* **El Salvador Tourist Card ($12 USD):** US and many foreign passport holders pay a **$12 USD tourist entry fee** upon arrival in El Salvador (payable in cash or card at immigration—no advance visa needed for US/EU/UK/CA citizens). *If you hold a passport that requires an advance visa for Guatemala or El Salvador, check consulate timelines at least 6 weeks out.*

### 4. Money & Payments

* **Guatemala (Guatemalan Quetzal — `GTQ`):** Cards are widely accepted at Antigua hotels, restaurants, and cafes, (~7.7–7.8 GTQ per $1 USD), but you **must have cash (`GTQ`)** for Acatenango park entrance fees, porter payments, tips, and street vendors. Bring a zero-fee debit card (like Charles Schwab or Fidelity) to pull Quetzales from an ATM inside a bank in Antigua.
* **El Salvador (US Dollar — `USD`):** El Salvador uses the **US Dollar** as its official currency (alongside Bitcoin, which is common in El Zonte). Bring **$100–$150 in crisp, untorn $1, $5, $10, and $20 US bills** from home—small mountain shops and waterfall guides in the Ruta de las Flores often cannot break a $50 or $100 bill, and torn bills are routinely rejected.

### 5. Gear & Packing Essentials

Because this trip spans **13,000-foot freezing volcanic ridges** and **88°F Pacific beaches**, layering is everything:

* **For Acatenango (Night lows of 20°F–32°F / -6°C–0°C):**
  * Broken-in trail runners or hiking boots with good tread (volcanic gravel is slippery on the descent)
  * Base layers (merino wool or synthetic top & bottom)
  * Fleece mid-layer + packable down puffer jacket + windproof/waterproof outer shell
  * Warm beanie, winter gloves, and a buff/neck gaiter (essential for volcanic dust)
  * Headlamp with spare charge (for camp and the 4:00 AM summit push)
  * *Note:* The outfitter provides sleeping bags, pads, cabins, and meals, and you can also rent heavy cold-weather jackets/gloves directly from their shop in Antigua if you don't want to pack winter gear for one night.
* **For Waterfalls & Coast (El Salvador):**
  * Second pair of trail runners or grippy water shoes (like Astral, Keen, or Teva) that can get completely submerged at **Siete Cascadas** and **Tamanique**
  * Swimwear, reef-safe sunscreen, sunglasses, and a small dry bag for your phone
* **For Remote Work Days:**
  * Laptop + compact USB-C charger
  * Noise-canceling earbuds/headphones for calls
  * An **eSIM** (Airalo, Holafly, or T-Mobile/Google Fi international roaming) activated before departure so you have instant mobile hotspot backup in both countries
* **Daypack:** A comfortable **20L–28L backpack** that works as your personal item on the plane, your light daypack on Acatenango (while your main luggage stays locked at our Antigua hotel), and your hike pack in El Salvador.
