## Hook

Discover the diverse landscapes of the Cascades just an hour from Seattle with a guide that balances comprehensive trail data with essential safety and etiquette tips for the modern outdoorsman.

## Overview

This book serves as a comprehensive and updated guide to the Snoqualmie Region, tailored for the 3 million residents of the Seattle metro area. It details 136 day hikes across diverse terrains, including the Cascade Foothills, I-90 corridor, Alpine Lakes, and new areas like Blewett Pass and Ellensburg Basin. Beyond route descriptions, the text emphasizes safety and ethics, covering trailhead crime prevention, 'Leave No Trace' principles, and specific advice for hiking with dogs. The book utilizes a difficulty rating system based on steepness and terrain rather than just distance, supported by maps, elevation profiles, and nature photography.

## Summary

The vast wilderness of Washington's Cascade Foothills and the I-90 corridor is accessible to millions, yet requires careful navigation and respect. In *Day Hiking: Snoqualmie Region*, Dan A. Nelson provides a comprehensive guide to over 136 day hikes that stretch from the Puget Sound to the Ellensburg Basin. As the author notes, the region offers 'stunning scenery' and some of the 'richest ecosystems' in the country, all within an hour of Seattle. The book serves as a practical tool for both novice and experienced hikers, featuring difficulty ratings, driving directions, and updates on trail conditions. Nelson, who has logged thousands of research miles, emphasizes that while the trails are a joy, they require responsibility. He addresses critical safety issues, ranging from trailhead crime prevention to wilderness etiquette. For those hiking with dogs, he advocates for strict leashing and preparedness, arguing that people will hike with pets regardless, so they must 'do it responsibly.' The guide also includes detailed logistics such as permit information, camping options, and natural history highlights. By balancing detailed maps with the freedom to explore, Nelson equips readers to discover diverse landscapes-from alpine meadows to deep forested valleys-while ensuring their safety and the preservation of the environment.

## Key ideas

### Explore the Expanded Backyard

Summary: The Snoqualmie Region offers unparalleled access to diverse ecosystems for Seattle residents, featuring over 136 updated hikes that cover new territories including Blewett Pass, Greenwater, and Ellensburg.

Why it matters: With the region's proximity to 3 million residents, knowing the full scope of available trails helps distribute foot traffic and allows hikers to experience varied landscapes, from alpine meadows to high desert steppes, without driving extreme distances.

Try this: Use the new eight-page color-photo insert and updated maps to select a hike in a newly added area, such as the Ellensburg Basin, that offers a different terrain experience from the usual Snoqualmie Pass corridors.

### Deciphering the Trails

Summary: The guide employs a difficulty rating system (1 to 5) not solely based on distance, but incorporating steepness, terrain type like talus slopes, and overall exertion required, providing a realistic expectation of the hike's challenge.

Why it matters: Relying solely on mileage can be misleading; a short, steep climb on loose rocks may be significantly more dangerous and tiring than a long, flat walk. Accurate ratings prevent hikers from getting in over their heads.

Try this: When choosing a hike, cross-reference the rating with the elevation profile charts provided in the book to ensure you are physically prepared for the specific steepness and surface conditions.

### Canine Companions and Trail Etiquette

Summary: Nelson advocates for responsible dog ownership on the trail, emphasizing that dogs should always be leashed and equipped with the proper gear, such as booties and waste disposal tools, to ensure safety and courtesy to others.

Why it matters: Unprepared pets can become a hazard or a nuisance. Nelson's approach acknowledges that dog hiking is inevitable, shifting the focus to minimizing impact on wildlife and other hikers through preparation and control.

Try this: If you hike with a dog, pack a dedicated 'trail kit' including water, a first aid kit, booties, and waste bags to ensure you can adhere to Nelson's standards of responsibility.

### Trailhead Safety Awareness

Summary: Urban encroachment has brought societal issues like car break-ins to trailheads. Nelson advises hikers to hide valuables, trust their intuition, and be willing to abandon a hike if the environment feels unsafe.

Why it matters: Safety begins before the hike starts. Most trailhead crimes are opportunistic; removing incentives helps protect personal property and ensures the day ends on a positive note.

Try this: Adopt a 'clean car' policy where you remove all visible items from your vehicle before parking, and if you see suspicious activity, record details and drive to a different trailhead immediately.

### The Ethics of Leaving No Trace

Summary: True wilderness stewardship involves strict adherence to 'Leave No Trace' principles, which include packing out all trash-even biodegradable items like apple cores-and purifying water from natural sources.

Why it matters: Leaving food scraps attracts wildlife and disrupts local ecosystems. Maintaining the pristine nature of the trails ensures they remain enjoyable and safe for future generations of hikers and animals.

Try this: Carry a dedicated trash bag for every hike and make it a rule to inspect your campsite or rest area before leaving to ensure nothing remains, regardless of how small or 'natural' the item is.

## Action steps

1. Plan trips using the updated guide which features checked and updated trails, new areas like Blewett Pass and Ellensburg, and enhanced photography.
2. Practice responsible hiking with dogs by keeping them leashed, carrying first aid and waste disposal gear, and using dog booties for rough terrain.
3. Prioritize safety at trailheads by hiding valuables, locking cars, and moving to another location if suspicious individuals are present.
4. Practice 'Leave No Trace' ethics by packing out all trash, including biodegradable items like apple cores, to preserve the trail environment.
5. Prepare for emergencies by carrying water purification methods and basic emergency shelter such as a space blanket or garbage bags.
6. Utilize the book's ratings (1-5), which assess difficulty based on length, steepness, and terrain complexity, rather than distance alone.
