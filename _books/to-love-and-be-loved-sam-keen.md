## Summary

### To Love and Be Loved

**Sam Keen**

#### Introduction: The Great Art of Love

We live in a culture obsessed with romance and intimacy, yet we remain dangerously ignorant of the full range of human love. We are constantly bombarded with tired self-help nostrums and bad advice that treats love as a game of luck or a passive emotion we simply "fall" into. Sam Keen challenges this notion head-on. He argues that love is not a destination or a sudden accident, but a complex art form. Like any great art, it combines a variety of skills and talents that require a lifetime to learn fully. The goal is not merely to find love but to become a person capable of giving and receiving it in all its forms. This pursuit is the "real work" of your life, essential for feeling rooted and secure in the world.

Keen sets out to clear away the confusion surrounding love by providing a "stunningly new map" of the emotional landscape. He mixes frank autobiographical recollections with insights from modern psychology and spiritual traditions. By doing so, he illustrates that love is the "way, the truth, and the life." It is the fundamental force that connects us to our families, our labors, and significant others. To hide from this knowledge is to live in fear; to embrace it is to begin the journey of becoming a lover.

#### The Sixteen Elements of Love

At the heart of Keen's philosophy is the idea that love is not a monolith. It is composed of sixteen distinct "elements." These components range from universal gifts we can give anyone to exclusive gifts reserved for intimacy. By breaking love down into these specific elements, Keen transforms a vague, mystical feeling into a tangible practice. Each element can be studied, practiced, and improved. The book moves through these elements one by one, concluding each section with practical suggestions for strengthening that specific skill in our daily lives.

##### Attention: The Precious Gift

The first and perhaps most fundamental element is attention. In a world full of distractions, giving someone your full focus is a rare and valuable gift. Attention is the act of noticing and focusing on another person. It is the prerequisite for all other forms of love. You cannot truly care for, empathize with, or enjoy someone if you have not first paid attention to them. This gift is not limited to our spouses or lovers. We can bestow attention on co-workers, friends, and children alike, signaling to them that they are seen and valued.

##### Empathy and Compassion

Keen moves from the outward act of attention to the internal dynamics of the heart. Empathy is described as the "loving imagination." It is the ability to project yourself into another person's experience, to understand their perspective from the inside out. Compassion takes this a step further into "sympathetic suffering." It is not just understanding someone's pain but being willing to suffer with them. These qualities are essential for moving beyond a self-centered existence. Cultivating empathy and compassion allows us to share the burdens and joys of others, creating deep bonds of trust and intimacy.

##### Knowledge and Sensuality

Love is not just about feeling; it is about knowing. Keen emphasizes "mindful love" or knowledge. This involves truly learning the other person, their history, their fears, and their dreams. This intellectual knowledge is paired with sensuality, which is "being in touch." Sensuality grounds love in the physical world. It reminds us that we are embodied creatures and that touch, presence, and physical awareness are vital languages of love. Together, knowledge and sensuality ensure that our love is both grounded in reality and deeply felt.

#### The Dynamics of Relationship

Beyond the individual elements of attention and feeling, Keen explores the structural components that make love last. He highlights the tension and balance between different forces that drive human connection.

##### Enjoyment vs. Commitment

A central theme in the book is the necessity of balancing enjoyment with commitment. Our culture often prioritizes the thrill of enjoyment and romantic attraction. However, Keen argues that true love requires the discipline of commitment. Enjoyment brings the spark and delight, but commitment provides the safety and stability needed to weather the inevitable storms of life. One without the other is unstable. A relationship based solely on enjoyment is fleeting, while a relationship based only on duty is dry and resentful. The art lies in maintaining the "will to love" through commitment while actively seeking "sympathetic pleasure" through enjoyment.

##### Memory and Storytelling

We are the stories we tell ourselves. In love, "storytelling" serves as the loving narrative. Shared memories and the stories we tell about them construct the history of a relationship. Memory acts as the glue that holds the past to the present. By remembering and retelling our shared history, we reinforce our identity as a couple or a family. Keen suggests that without this shared narrative, love loses its depth. We must actively tend to our stories, remembering the highs and learning from the lows, to maintain a sense of continuity and meaning.

#### The Necessity of Solitude and Self-Love

Ironically, one of the most important elements of loving others is the ability to be alone. Keen dedicates significant attention to the concepts of "self-love" and "solitude." He frames solitude not as the opposite of companionship, but as "solitary communion." It is a time to reconnect with oneself, to nurture one's own spirit, and to find a sense of groundedness independent of others.

Self-love is the foundation upon which all other love is built. It is not narcissism, but a healthy appreciation for one's own worth. Keen argues that you cannot give what you do not have. If you are alienated from yourself, your attempts to love others will often be needy, manipulative, or exhausting. By practicing solitude and self-love, we become full individuals who can love out of abundance rather than deficit.

#### The Erotic and the Divine

Keen does not shy away from the physical aspects of love. He discusses "desire" and "sexuality" as exclusive gifts that deepen intimacy. However, he places these within a much larger context. The erotic is not just about sex; it is about passion, creativity, and the life force. It is the energy that drives us to connect and create.

This physical energy is ultimately connected to the spiritual. The final section of the book is a soaring meditation on the claim that "those who love know God." Keen invites readers to experience their place in the universe through the eyes of love. Love is the "commonwealth of being." It is the force that dissolves the illusion that we are separate entities. When we love, we participate in something greater than ourselves. This spiritual perspective transforms the mundane acts of daily life into acts of devotion. It suggests that loving a neighbor, a child, or a partner is a way of touching the divine.

#### Conclusion: The Practice of Love

To Love and Be Loved is ultimately a call to action. It asks us to stop waiting for love to happen to us and start practicing it with the same dedication we would apply to learning a musical instrument or a craft. Keen outlines the elements, from adoration and care to repentance and forgiveness, showing us where we need to grow.

The destinty Keen proposes is not just to find love, but to be the most loving person you can be. When we feel something is missing in a relationship, it is often because we are failing to bring our full selves to it. By mastering the elements of attention, empathy, commitment, and compassion, we enlarge our ability to love. In doing so, we find the security and rootedness we all seek. Love is the way home. It is the path to meaning, the path to connection, and the path to the divine. It requires courage to overcome our fears and discipline to master the art, but the reward is a life truly lived.
