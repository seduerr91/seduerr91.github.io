## Hook

Unlock the ability to perform lightning-fast calculations in your head by shifting your strategy from right-to-left to left-to-right and learning the 'mathemagician's' shortcuts for complex problems.

## Overview

This book is a comprehensive guide to performing mental calculations quickly and accurately. It teaches readers how to reorient their approach to arithmetic, advocating for left-to-right processing and simplification strategies. The text covers a wide range of topics, including rapid addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, as well as advanced techniques for squaring numbers and estimation. Furthermore, it delves into cognitive tools like the Major System for memorizing numbers, providing readers with a toolkit to improve their computational fluency and confidence.

## Summary

In "Secrets of Mental Math," Arthur Benjamin demonstrates that mental arithmetic is not an innate talent but a learnable skill that relies on simplification and strategy. The core philosophy involves processing numbers from left to right, which aligns with how we read and allows us to keep running totals mentally. Benjamin introduces specific techniques to streamline addition, such as adding hundreds before tens, and simplifies squaring numbers ending in five with a quick trick: multiply the first digit by the next higher digit and append 25. Beyond calculation, the book covers methods for estimation, division, and memory. It introduces the Major System, a phonetic code that turns numbers into words, making it easier to remember phone numbers or dates. Ultimately, mastering these strategies boosts confidence, sharpens cognitive abilities, and equips readers to handle everyday math tasks-like calculating tips or verifying bills-quickly and accurately.

## Key ideas

### Calculate Left to Right

Summary: The most efficient way to perform mental math is to calculate from left to right, dealing with the largest place values first. This contrasts with the pencil-and-paper method of right-to-left calculation.

Why it matters: Processing numbers in the order they are read makes it easier to manage the calculation mentally and provides a running total that is easier to retain.

Try this: When adding 538 and 327, first add the hundreds (500 + 300 = 800), then the tens (30 + 20 = 50 for 850), and finally the ones (8 + 7 = 15) to reach 865.

### Simplify Through Decomposition

Summary: Complex arithmetic problems should be simplified by breaking them down into smaller, more manageable components or 'chunks.'

Why it matters: Breaking problems down reduces cognitive load and makes seemingly difficult mental calculations, like finding squares or adding large numbers, surprisingly straightforward.

Try this: When squaring a number that ends in 5, such as 35, ignore the 5 initially. Multiply the first digit (3) by the next higher digit (4) to get 12. Then, simply append 25 to the result to get 1225.

### Use the Math of Least Resistance

Summary: Mental math involves choosing the path of least resistance. The book advises looking for features in numbers-such as proximity to a base of 10 or 100-to simplify the operation.

Why it matters: Rounding numbers or using complements (e.g., turning subtraction into addition) significantly speeds up estimation and calculation in daily situations.

Try this: If you need to multiply 69 by itself, round it up to 70. Perform the calculation based on this easier number, then adjust your result to account for the difference.

### Verify with Nines and Boost Memory

Summary: To ensure accuracy and memorize information, use 'casting out nines' for verification and the Major System for memory.

Why it matters: These tools allow you to check your work for errors without a calculator and retain long strings of numerical data, such as phone numbers or historical dates, with ease.

Try this: To remember a number like 32, use the Major System to translate digits into consonants (3 = M, 2 = N), then add vowels to create a memorable image like 'Moon' or 'Man.'

## Action steps

1. Adopt a left-to-right approach for all mental calculations to align with natural reading flow and keep partial answers available.
2. Break down complex problems into simpler, manageable parts rather than attempting to process the entire number at once.
3. Utilize specific shortcuts for numbers ending in 5 by multiplying the leading digit by the next highest digit and appending 25.
4. Verify your calculations using the 'casting out nines' method to ensure accuracy without using a calculator.
5. Memorize long numbers using the Major System by converting digits into consonant sounds and forming them into words or stories.
