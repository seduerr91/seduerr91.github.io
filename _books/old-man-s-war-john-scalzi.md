## Summary

### The False Promise of Youth
Humanity has finally achieved interstellar travel, but the reality is far from a utopian dream. The universe is heavily populated, and planets capable of sustaining life are scarce. Consequently, space-faring species compete aggressively for these resources. To defend Earth and stake a claim on planetary real estate, the Colonial Union relies on the Colonial Defense Force (CDF). The CDF does not recruit young soldiers; instead, they seek the elderly. At 75, citizens can enlist, trading their old bodies for a chance to fight in the stars. In return for two years of hazardous service, survivors are granted a generous homestead stake on a colony planet.

John Perry, a 75-year-old retired advertising writer, takes this deal. Having signed a letter of intent with his late wife Kathy a decade prior, Perry visits her grave to say goodbye, knowing he can never return to Earth. He boards a space elevator to the CDF ship *Henry Hudson*, where he meets fellow retirees. They bond over their shared history and dub themselves the "Old Farts," unaware of the true transformation that awaits them.

### A Second Life
The mystery of how the elderly can fight is solved when the CDF reveals its technology. Perry undergoes a procedure where his consciousness is transplanted into a clone of himself. However, this is not merely a restoration of youth. His new body is genetically engineered for combat, featuring enhanced musculature, green skin, and yellow cat-like eyes. He possesses nanobot-enhanced artificial blood, enhanced senses, and a BrainPal-a neural interface that allows for instant communication with other soldiers.

Perry and his fellow recruits enjoy a brief period of libertinism in their new bodies, experiencing a physical vitality they had not felt in decades. However, the frivolity ends quickly. They are shipped to Beta Pyxis III for basic training, a brutal regimen grounded in the traditions of the United States armed forces. Perry, leveraging his past life experience, manages to gain the respect of the drill instructor and is appointed platoon leader.

### The School of Hard Knocks
Combat is immediate and varied. Perry's first engagement is against the Consu, a species known for their intelligence and religious zeal. While the CDF relies on advanced technology, Perry finds that his most potent weapon is his lifetime of experience. He improvises tactics that outmaneuver alien strategies, securing victory for his unit.

Subsequent battles involve diverse species, from the bear-like Whaidians to the tiny Covandu. Perry rises through the ranks, but the cost is high. The "Old Farts" begin to die one by one. The attrition rate is staggering, and the reality of endless warfare sets in. Perry becomes increasingly dissatisfied with the CDF's approach, feeling that the universe's hostility makes peace impossible.

### A Ghost from the Past
The psychological toll culminates during the Battle for Coral. This engagement against the Rraey-a species that eats humans-is a disaster. The CDF is ambushed, largely because the Rraey have acquired tachyon-based skip-drive detection technology from the Consu. Perry is grievously wounded and believes he is dying when he sees his dead wife, Kathy, standing over him.

The figure is actually Jane Sagan, the leader of the Special Forces unit known as the Ghost Brigades. Sagan was grown from Kathy's DNA using the letter of intent she signed before her death. Because Kathy died before she could join, Sagan has no memories of her life as a human and is a distinct entity. Despite the lack of shared history, Perry is forced to confront the physical reality of his wife's genetic duplicate and the complex nature of identity.

### Identity and Humanity
As Perry recovers, he learns that the CDF operates on a philosophy of utility. The Ghost Brigades, created from the DNA of people who died before enlisting, are considered superior weapons because they have no family ties or past lives to distract them. Jane Sagan, however, is curious about the origin of her DNA. She seeks to understand what it means to be a "realborn" person.

This relationship challenges Perry's understanding of what makes him human. His mind is old, but his body is manufactured. His wife is dead, yet a version of her lives. Scalzi uses these elements to explore themes of life extension and individuality. Perry realizes that despite his altered biology, his humanity is preserved through his memories, his relationships, and his moral choices.

### Improvisation in the Void
To redeem the failure at Coral, Perry joins Sagan on a special operation to destroy the Consu technology that allowed the ambush. In the ensuing battle to retake Coral, Perry once again relies on improvisation rather than standard protocol. He successfully captures the technical manual for the detection system and saves Sagan's life after she is critically wounded.

Perry's actions highlight the novel's central theme: the irreplaceable value of human experience. In a universe filled with high-tech weaponry and genetically modified soldiers, the ability to think laterally and adapt to the unknown-skills honed over a lifetime-remains the deciding factor in survival.

### The Cost of a New Start
By the conclusion of the novel, Perry is promoted to the rank of captain. He has survived his initial term, though the war is far from over. He manages to leverage his influence to move his few remaining friends out of active combat roles, protecting them from the meat-grinder of front-line duty.

Although he is separated from Jane Sagan, who returns to the secretive Ghost Brigades, Perry holds onto hope for the future. The novel closes with the understanding that while the CDF can remake the body, the mind remains anchored to its history. Perry's journey is not just a fight against aliens, but a struggle to retain his humanity and identity in the face of radical physical and technological change.
